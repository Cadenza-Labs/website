The goal of our group is to do research which contributes to AI Safety. **Our main goal is to research, benchmark and develop robust lie detectors for LLMs.** 

Our research priorities are described in our [roadmap](/).

## Latest Work

### [Liars' Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16035)
Prior work has introduced techniques for detecting when large language models (LLMs) lie, that is, generating statements they believe are false. However, these techniques are typically validated in narrow settings that do not capture the diverse lies LLMs can generate. We introduce LIARS' BENCH, a testbed consisting of 72,863 examples of lies and honest responses generated by four open-weight models across seven datasets. Our settings capture qualitatively different types of lies and vary along two dimensions: the model's reason for lying and the object of belief targeted by the lie. Evaluating three black- and white-box lie detection techniques on LIARS' BENCH, we find that existing techniques systematically fail to identify certain types of lies, especially in settings where it's not possible to determine whether the model lied from the transcript alone. Overall, LIARS' BENCH reveals limitations in prior techniques and provides a practical testbed for guiding progress in lie detection. The benchmark is available on [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Cadenza-Labs/liars-bench).

The paper was accepted at the NeurIPS 2026 conference. Liars' Bench has been used and cited by several AI safety groups: Anthropic's Alignment Science team [based its Harm Pressure setting on ours](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2025/honesty-elicitation/) and used our taxonomy to select its testbeds; a [UK AISI paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12618) compares against our model organisms; a [Google DeepMind paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22662) engages with our datasets in depth; and a [LASR Labs/UK AISI team](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01425) and FAR.AI's [Obfuscation Atlas](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15515) evaluate on our data.

### [Aletheia's Quest: An AI Lie Detection Competition](https://aletheias-quest.github.io/)
We were scientific leads for Aletheia's Quest, an AI lie detection competition organized together with [NDIF](https://ndif.us) and supported by Schmidt Sciences. As we and others have shown, existing lie detectors have clear limitations; this (semi-)open competition brought together researchers with the goal of inventing new methods that reliably detect lies across a wide range of scenarios. We designed the competition rules and were responsible for the evaluation datasets generated by model organisms: models that were prompted to lie and others that lied in a more natural way. The datasets were created by external teams under our supervision, and some we designed and created ourselves (e.g. [Echoblast](https://huggingface.co/Cadenza-Labs/echoblast-qwen3.5-27b)). You can think of Aletheia's Quest as a scaled-up successor to Liars' Bench, with new datasets generated by many teams and many lie detectors created by the participants.

We are currently finalizing the post-competition report. In the meantime, check out the [datasets and model organisms](https://huggingface.co/collections/aletheias-quest/aletheias-quest-datasets-and-model-organisms) we created for the competition.

### [The Steerability Challenge: NeurIPS Honesty Steering Competition](https://steerability.github.io)
We are teaming up with researchers from IBM and Tara Research to host a NeurIPS competition for interventions that make models produce honest outputs, which started in September 2026. We contribute by shaping the rules and design for a fair and useful competition, and by creating model organisms. This is the second competition we are co-hosting, after Aletheia's Quest.

### [Cluster-Norm for Unsupervised Probing of Knowledge](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.780/)
This method addresses challenges in identifying relevant knowledge features amidst distracting features in model activations. By clustering and normalizing activation patterns before applying probing techniques, the approach enhances the accuracy of unsupervised probes in extracting intended knowledge. While not addressing all limitations of current methods, cluster normalization shows promise in making unsupervised probing more robust and reliable for understanding the knowledge encoded in language models.

This paper was accepted at the MechInterp workshop at ICML 2024 and the EMNLP 2024 conference. 

### [AI-AI Bias](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2415697122)
This work was done in collaboration with [ACS](https://acsresearch.org/about). We conducted experiments to test whether large language models (LLMs) like GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 show bias when selecting between items described by AI-generated content versus human-authored content. Using product descriptions and academic paper abstracts, we found LLMs consistently preferred to select items where the descriptions or abstracts were generated by other LLMs, rather than those written by humans. This suggests a potential "anti-human" bias in AI systems that could disadvantage human workers if LLMs are increasingly used for decision-making in economic contexts. Our study raises concerns about fairness and discrimination as AI becomes more integrated into various aspects of society and the economy.

this paper was published in the PNAS journal in 2025.
